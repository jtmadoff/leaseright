**One recommendation.** Keep the owner command center as the *eventual*
product. Do **not** authorize a 60-day operating-system + originator pilot
as the first paid thing. Sell a **paid Model reconstruction + weekly
variance memo** on one named lease-up, from the sponsor's own records.

share is the intended P&L and is **small, late, and ICP-fragile** — qualify

## 1. Independent position

The original thesis is still false. The peer consensus absorbed that and
then overfit.

Four claims, in order of how much they break *what the company is about to
authorize*:

2. **The calculator is no longer a loaded velocity ranking. It is still

originator Stage 1" is the new morality play: a more sophisticated way to
build the collaborator before the buyer.

## 2. Does the model work?

### 2.1 What "the model" is, as written

From `PRODUCT_DIRECTION.md`:

- Buyer = developer / small-to-mid portfolio owner.
- Wedge = pre-funding lender-ready lease-up plan.
- Operating layer = Today / Pipeline / Inbox / Rents / Applications.
- Economics = **`$1 per unit forever`** for software; real money from
  **payments, deposits, ACH/card fees, banking**.
- Best-case staffing = in-house leasing; brokers are a "realistic
  transitional" guest; PMs are "common but less ideal."

From Justin's confirmed synthesis (`spec/40_FOUNDATION_SYNTHESIS.md`): owner
signs up first, invites collaborators, visibility flows up, never sideways;
Lead Broker exists only on the broker-assisted route; lender is export-only.

From the 2026-09-01 peer stack: that story does not work; replace it with a
paid owner command center, hybrid default, originator rail, five-figure
60-day pilot, freeze N3–N17.

The first half of the peer diagnosis is right. The replacement commercial
act is still too much product for a company with no customer.

### 2.2 The `$1/unit` layer cannot carry the wedge

On The Meridian (the only fully modeled project):

| Line | Source | Amount |
|------|--------|--------|
| Software at `$1/unit` (as currently written) | `PRODUCT_DIRECTION` + 260 units | **$260 / year** if read as per unit per year; **undefined** if "forever" has no period |
| Software at the *archived* price `$1/unit/month` | `Archive/LeaseUp_prototypes` copy | **$3,120 / year** |
| Monthly carry | `model-data.jsx` `budget.carryCostPerMonth` | **$227,000 / month** |
| Base-case carry across lease-up | `scenarios` `sc-base.carryCost` | **$1.71M** |
| Two weeks of delay | 0.5 × $227K | **$113,500** |

The current UI (`shell.jsx`) still says `owner · $1/unit · forever`. The
superseded LeaseUp prototypes said `$1/unit/month`. The billing period was
dropped, not resolved. Even restoring `/month` leaves software revenue at
~$3K/year against $227K/month of carry. A two-week slip in absorption eats
any staffing-savings story the calculator can currently produce.

The docs already admit the lease-up workflow is the wedge and payments are
the engine. The problem is **when** that engine turns on.

Payments require occupied units. The Meridian seed is 121/260 leased (~47%)
mid-lease-up. A 1% of collected rent as processing take, at
stabilization (~242 occupied × $2,180 × 12 × 1%), is on the order of **$63K /
year**. **Withdrawn as a processing-fee case** on 2026-09-02
(`spec/CONTRARIAN_PAYMENTS_ENGINE.md`): 1% of rent is a PM skim; a share of
processing fees on the seed's 74% free-ACH mix is ~$0–$16K/year, and the
demo ledger already books Stripe as a cost. Remaining structural problems:

- It arrives *after* the lease-up the product is selling into.
- Merchant-builders (called out in `10_PERSONAS` §1.4) sell at
  stabilization. The payment relationship transfers with the asset. The
  long-term-holder default the personas spec recommends is the segment where
  the thesis pays off, not the segment the prototype's "lender package" wedge
  is aimed at.
- Winning payments means beating RealPage / AppFolio / the PM's existing
  ACH stack *after* a PM is often already hired. That is a fintech company
  with money-transmitter and bank-partner work, not a static HTML prototype
  decision.
- The seed's `payments[]` are mock rows. `selectors.jsx` staffing actual is
  the plan cost echoed back because "live payroll/commission is not yet a
  first-class entity."

**Verdict on the business model:** the wedge (Model) and the engine
(payments) are still separated by a year of operations `$1/unit` cannot
fund and payments cannot yet monetize. Something in the middle has to be
a paid product. The cheapest honest version of that product is **calibrated
underwriting + a weekly owner memo**, not a connected leasing OS.

`$1/unit forever` is also a **category error**. Per-unit-forever is
property-management seat pricing (the category LeaseRight claims not to be).
Lease-up is a 6–18 month project. Price the project. Do not then inflate
"project" into "operate the building for 60 days."

| Staffing | Owner or one on-site person can actually run it | On-site office is necessary, not sufficient |
| Agents | Optional; "fire the broker" is plausible | Locators are a demand channel; exclusive brokers may already be hired |

### 2.6 The 60-day OS pilot cannot falsify the software thesis

This is the claim the other memos underweight.

The Overseer Stage 1 bundle tests, at once: sponsor payment, operator
adoption of seven surfaces, mechanism proxies, originator registrations,
and a lender reaction. Failures will not tell you *which* hypothesis died.
Successes will not tell you the software did it. A 60-day engagement with
Prospeer in the room tests whether Justin can run a lease-up desk, not
whether LeaseRight is a product.

The Strategist is right that willingness-to-pay measures belief, and that
one building cannot prove a network. The Strategist is wrong that the fix
is **two buildings** and **carry-share pricing** before a single sponsor
has paid for a memo. Two unfalsifiable pilots are not more scientific than
one. Carry-share against a Model that disagrees with itself by $148K–$277K
per scenario is a more sophisticated version of the original calculator
lie.

The Implementer is right that a public prototype teaching false fee math
is worse than waiting a day to relabel. That day already happened. The
remaining lie is duration and leftover intake copy. Fixing those is still
not a reason to stand up originator registration.

**Falsifiable first commercial test:** a named sponsor pays for a
reconciled baseline and one weekly variance memo, produced from *their*
records, without requiring their leasing office to live in LeaseRight.
If they will not pay for that, they will not pay for an OS. If they will,
you have a paid middle and a Model worth taking into ops later.

## 3. How agents actually fit — and why the current role will fail

### 3.1 The product conflates four different people

`PRODUCT_DIRECTION`, `10_PERSONAS`, and the role enum
(`owner | asset_manager | leasing_agent | broker | property_manager |
lender_observer`) treat "broker" as one external collaborator. The market
does not.

| Actor | Who they are | How they get paid | What they need from software |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|------------------------------|
| **On-site / in-house leasing agent** (Priya) | W-2 or contract staff of the sponsor or PM | Salary / bonus on units leased | A daily workbench: leads, tours, apps. This is the current Pipeline/Today design, and it is the right product *for this person*. |
| **Exclusive listing / lease-up broker** | Brokerage hired to run the whole lease-up | Flat, % of first-year, or per-unit on the exclusive | They already have a CRM and a process. They will not become a guest in the owner's scorecard. They are a *buyer* of lease-up software, or a competitor. |
| **Apartment locator / tenant-rep agent** | Independent or shop agent who brings a renter to *this* building | Landlord-paid commission on that lease, often 50–100% of one month **[market hypothesis]** | Commission registration, protected client, fast apply, fast pay. They will not accept an SLA clock. They will use whatever gets their client in first. |
| **Residential Realtor** (relocating buyer who rents first) | Licensed agent with a relocating client | Often a locator-like referral fee, sometimes nothing | A sendable application / tour link that makes *them* look competent to their client. |

The party question is how real estate agents create trust, distribution, and
revenue without adding friction. The current canonical answer — owner
invites a `broker` into scoped guest access, we measure them, we keep them
off Model and money — is written for the exclusive listing broker the
sponsor wants to fire. The consensus answer — originator rail in Stage 1 —
is written for a locator who does not yet have a building to place into.

Trust, in the guest design, flows only one way: the owner trusts the system
to police the agent. Agents create trust by *not getting their client stolen
by the on-site office* and by looking competent to the renter. Distribution
is them sending the next client. Revenue is their commission, optionally a
take-rate. Friction is a second CRM whose purpose is the landlord's
visibility. The current *specified* role inverts all four. The consensus
rail gets the shape right and the sequencing wrong.

### 3.2 Why they will not log into the product as designed

The designed broker loop (`10_PERSONAS` §2.3, §5):

1. Owner creates the project alone.
2. Owner picks in-house / broker-assisted / PM at launch.
3. If broker-assisted, owner *invites* brokers into guest workspaces.
4. Broker sees only assigned leads, logs tours, pushes applications.
5. Owner sees a performance scorecard.

Failure points, in practice:

- **GTM is inverted.** **[market hypothesis]:** on a 200+ unit lease-up, the
  exclusive broker is often hired *before* any software. The owner does not
  invite the broker into LeaseRight; the broker tells the owner which ILS
  and CRM they use. "Owner signs up first" (`D7`) is right for the Model
  wedge and fatal for exclusive-broker adoption.
- **Double entry is a veto.** Locators live in Follow Up Boss, kvCORE,
  BoomTown, spreadsheet + text, or association tools. A second login whose
  purpose is the landlord's visibility is unpaid work.
- **Scorecards are adversarial.** Copy can say "not anti-broker in the UI."
  The data model says the owner-facing artifact is a scorecard that "exposes
  underperforming brokers." Agents talk. One burned shop and the locator
  network routes around the building.
- **Commission attribution without a pay rail is a report, not a reason to
  show up.** There is nothing for an agent to *get*.
- **Row-scoping the pipeline hides the inventory they need.** A locator
  cannot sell a unit they cannot see. "Assigned leads only" makes sense for
  an exclusive broker running *your* leads. It makes no sense for an agent
  who arrives with *their* client and needs the live rent/availability
  matrix.
- **A timestamp the landlord controls is not evidence.** The Strategist is
  right on this amendment. The on-site office is the party most likely to
  convert the agent's client as a walk-in. Asking the agent to trust the
  landlord's database to adjudicate a claim *against the landlord's office*
  is the scorecard failure arriving through a different door.

### 3.3 Practical agent-role design (shape if they participate; not Stage 1)

Do not put locators or Realtors in the role matrix. Do not give them Today.
Do not row-scope Pipeline for them. `70_REBUILD_PLAN` Q4 becomes moot.

**Operator (login):** whoever works the building's own leads — Priya, a PM
leasing person, or (if the buyer is an exclusive shop) that shop's
leasing staff using the same workbench. One workbench, owner-owned data.

**Demand partner (no login, or magic link only):**

1. Public or authenticated **live availability** — unit type, asking,
   concession, commission terms. The Rents matrix as a feed, not
   view-only-after-invite. Terms published *before* registration.
2. **Register client + unit interest.** Timestamped protection against the
   on-site office claiming the walk-in. Rules visible at register. Terms in
   force are frozen for that claim.
3. **The agent and sponsoring brokerage hold the evidence.** Immediate
   confirmation (email/SMS) to both, outside LeaseRight's database. A
   landlord-held timestamp is not enough.
4. **Client-facing link** the agent forwards: tour book, apply, status.
   The agent looks good. The agent does not need F-keys.
5. **Milestone status only:** registered → toured → applied → leased →
   commission approved → paid, plus dispute outcome and reason.
6. **Pay them through the owner's actual rail** in v1 (outside the product);
   show owed / approved / paid-on-date and **days-to-pay**. Fast, uncontested
   pay is the only SLA agents want. Do not take a commission share in the
   first test. In Texas, the sponsoring brokerage is the default payee
   **[market / legal — counsel before any real money]**.
7. Owner command center sees `source=locator`, cost per lease, conversion,
   and 3BR overflow share. That is accountability without a scorecard aimed
   at a person who never agreed to be an employee.

**Sequencing constraint — this is the dissent.** Do not build this rail
before there is a building agents want to place into **and a sponsor who
has already paid for the Model**. An empty registration prototype is still
theater. The first agent test is a conversation, not a Typeform bolted onto
a 60-day OS. Five locator interviews can kill or qualify the rail without
importing TREC, dispute, and payout design.

**Exclusive listing brokers:** do not invite them as guests. Either sell
them the workbench (inverts D7; they become the buyer) or treat their
activity as an integration/import. Building Lead Broker as specified is
how the MVP grows a role matrix before a single agent has a reason to
show up. Building an originator mock "because the consensus wants a thin
surface" is the same mistake with less RBAC.

The Strategist's take-rate table is the right cold water on my earlier
implication that commission is the big pool. At 20–50% locator share, a
10% take is **$5.7K–$14K per building** — less than a $25K project fee.
Take-rate wins only on *repetition across buildings*. That is a network
argument. It should not fund Stage 1, and it cannot be observed until
there is a second building *and* a first one that already works. Do not
front-load the network test.

## 4. Overlooked alternatives

Ranked by how much of the current thesis they save vs. throw away.

**A. Paid Model reconstruction first (recommended).** Keep the owner-led
command center as the destination. Do not sell it as the first SKU. Charge
for reconciling one real lease-up's cost stack and delivering a weekly
variance memo. Add the locator rail only after a paying sponsor and a
hard-unit stall make overflow economically obvious. This is alternative B
from the prior brief, promoted: it is no longer "do less later." It is the
commercial sequence.

**B. Locator rail, not broker guest (right shape, wrong time).** The
consensus role split is correct. It is not the next build. Hybrid as
*observed origination share* matches the seed's staffing note and the 3BR
problem. Hybrid as *default company strategy* does not.

**C. Sell the exclusive broker as the buyer.** Lease-up brokerages run many
buildings. They already feel the spreadsheet / Sunday-email pain. Make
*them* the primary user of Pipeline; give the sponsor the Model + Reports
read. This is closer to how Yardi/RealPage entered the category. It
inverts D7 and kills the anti-broker framing. It also makes `$1/unit` even
harder, because the buyer is used to paying for ops software. **Nobody in
the peer stack is testing this**, because owner-first feels like Justin's
CRE identity. Two exclusive-broker conversations are cheaper than freezing
it out of the design.

**D. Charge for the Model. Stop promising `$1/unit` as the story.** Still
right. Do not then let "per-project fee" inflate into a 60-day operating
engagement that cannot distinguish product from service. `$1/unit` can
remain a land-and-expand number once ops are live.

**E. Productize overflow; stop calling in-house *or hybrid* the best case.**
The calculator should not default to anything. Origination share is an
input. In-house velocity should not be hardcoded (Builder already removed
this). Carry of delay sits on the same card as staffing savings (Builder
partially did this). User Modes §1–§3 collapse into one model with a
slider. A slower "cheap" plan can lose. An authored 40% hybrid can lose
too.

**F. Zero-login agent path.** Magic-link tour confirm, SMS "logged," public
application URL, email commission statement copied to the brokerage. No
guest workspace, no role-specific Today, no row-scoping engineering. Build
only after A clears.

**G. Carry-share pricing (Strategist).** Reject for pilot one. The Model
cannot currently produce a defensible delta. Quoting a fraction of a
contradictory number teaches the same false precision the old calculator
did. A fixed fee for a memo is honest about ignorance. After three paid
reconstructions, a published schedule can be discussed. Do not make Justin
choose "services vs network" before a single check clears.

## 5. What would change my mind

I am wrong, or partly wrong, if:

1. **Five recent 100–400 unit lease-ups** in Austin / Nashville / Denver
   (the prototype's own cities) leased to plan with an on-site person and
   *without* paying locators or an exclusive. Not anecdotes from sponsors
   who *wish* they had; absorption vs. their original lender model.
2. **A named sponsor refuses to pay for a Model memo** and will only pay
   if the seven-surface OS is included. Then the 60-day bundle is the
   actual SKU, not an overfit. Until that sentence is spoken by a buyer,
   it is a team preference.
3. **A locator shop agrees in writing** to work inside a landlord-owned
   portal because commission registration + faster pay is worth the
   friction, *and* there is a live building with published terms. Precedent
   exists in bits of Entrata / Funnel / EliseAI; it is not proven for a new
   brand with no inventory network.
4. **One lender** treats a LeaseRight-style package as more credible than
   the sponsor's Excel + broker opinion, enough to change a term or a
   reserve. Without that, the pre-funding wedge is a nicer spreadsheet.
5. **A payments partner residual is quoted** (not a from-scratch
   transmitter) so the economic engine is a $/txn or bps number, not a
   thesis. That still does not make one Meridian a company; see
   `spec/CONTRARIAN_PAYMENTS_ENGINE.md` §6.
6. **The target flips** from ground-up lease-up to small-portfolio unit
   turns. In-house agents and "fire the broker" are more realistic on
   8–40 unit assets. That is a different product than The Meridian demo.

Until 1 or 3 is true, building broker guest access, an owner scorecard, *or
an originator registration surface* is premature. Until 2 is true, do not
authorize a 60-day OS as the first paid product. Until 4 is true, do not bet
the company on the lender-package front door. Until 5 is true, do not use
payments to paper over the `$1/unit` gap. Until 6 is true, do not use
"small portfolio" language to justify a 260-unit in-house (or hybrid)
morality play.

## 6. Opposing views — and their likely weakness

### 6.1 The canonical thesis

> Developers hate brokerage opacity and PM bloat. Get them in before
> AppFolio with a lender-ready model. Support in-house, broker-assisted,
> and PM-managed so we don't force a staffing change. Keep brokers off
> the money, measure them, and the owner will adopt. `$1/unit` gets us in;
> payments make us a business. In-house is the best case because that's
> where we own the stack.

This view is attractive because it is how a sophisticated sponsor *talks*.
Jordan Mori, as written, is allergic to $283K of commission and weekly
broker calls. The prototype is a good mirror of that desire.

**Likely weakness:** it takes the buyer's *complaint* as the market's
*constraint*. Sponsors complain about brokers the way every founder
complains about recruiters — and still use them when the alternative is
an empty building and $227K/month carry. The docs already know the GTM
risk ("asking developers to change staffing behavior too early") and then
defuse it by supporting three operating models at once. Supporting three
models is how the MVP grows a role matrix, row-scoping, scorecards, and
PM containment before a single agent has a reason to log in.

The residue after the Builder patch: the velocity ranking is gone, but
in-house is still the cheap line, hybrid is still the recommended intake
card, and `$1/unit forever` is still in the chrome.

### 6.2 The Overseer / Builder / Implementer consensus (peer, 2026-09-01)

The memos correctly refuse to treat the calculator, the lender package,
`$1/unit`, or payments as proof. They land on hybrid default and a locator
registration loop. That overlap is not agreement that a 60-day OS pilot is
the next SKU.

**Likely weakness:** diplomacy that preserves too much architecture, now
repackaged as "validation."

- "Credible hypothesis" / "provisional go for a paid validation pilot" is
  a restatement of "the docs are coherent and the prototype is pretty."
  Coherence is not evidence. There is still **no customer, no paid intent,
  no lender behavior, no agent behavior** in this repository.
- "Paid validation pilot" as the next milestone still assumes the
  seven-surface OS is the thing to validate. Willingness-to-pay and locator
  behavior can be tested with a memo and interviews. A connected MVP is
  what you build *after* those answers, not the instrument you use to get
  them. Requiring 80% of leads through LeaseRight for four weeks is how a
  prototype becomes a staffing plan for Prospeer.
- Keeping owner-first for the underwriting wedge while "not assuming it
  governs every execution route" is having it both ways. D7, first-run, and
  the rebuild plan encode owner-first as architecture. If exclusive brokers
  are a real buyer, that is a different first-run, not a footnote.
- "A payment rail can follow only after registration behavior is proven"
  still treats pay as a later nice-to-have *and* still puts registration
  in Stage 1. For locators, pay *is* the product. Attribution without a
  pay path is a report. Registration without inventory is a form.
- Hybrid as test default, with 40% authored share, is still choosing a
  winner. The 11.3-day warning the Builder added is the tell: the ranking
  is unstable, so it should not be a default.
- Gate 0 (reconcile the Model from sponsor source data) is the right
  instrument. It does not require, and is diluted by, operator-adoption and
  originator-registration gates on the same 60-day clock.

Where the consensus is right: do not choose operating model by ideology;
separate the workbench user from the demand partner; Justin must pick an
ICP before more role engineering; do not run N7 as written; do not continue
N3–N17 as the next engineering. Those should be adopted as constraints,
not as permission to keep building the current golden path under a new
name.

### 6.3 The Strategist (peer, 2026-09-01)

Right that the Model is the pricing instrument, that timestamp-as-trust is
insufficient, that take-rate is a network bet, and that willingness-to-pay
is belief. Wrong on the commercial sequence those insights imply.

**Likely weakness:** using a better diagnosis to justify a *larger* first
test.

- Carry-share pricing against an incoherent Model repeats the calculator
  error at a higher register. The attribution fight the Strategist wants
  to avoid ("you didn't cause that") starts the moment the fee is a
  fraction of a carry delta the seed cannot currently compute.
- Two-building geometry answers a network question the company has not
  earned. Repeat-originator rate is the right metric *after* a paying
  sponsor and a working rail. It is not a reason to delay the first
  invoice or to recruit agents into a product that does not exist.
- Asking Justin to choose "services vs network" now is a false urgency.
  That choice is made by whether a second building reuses originators.
  It cannot be made in a memo, and it should not be made before the
  first check.

If other agents argue "we can empower brokers in the UI while the owner
gets the scorecard," the tell is the word *while*. If they argue "we can
validate the OS, the operator, and the originator in one paid 60 days,"
the tell is the word *and*.

   stays in the UI. Do not authorize a 60-day OS as the first SKU.

   apply → "commission owed," with evidence the agent and brokerage hold —
   not "invite broker → scoped pipeline → scorecard." If they are not yet
   in scope, do not build either.

### What to test next (cheapest evidence, in order)

1. **Reconstruct one completed or in-progress 100–400u lease-up** in a
   prototype city, *as a paid engagement*: weekly absorption vs original
   lender model, payroll, exclusive fees, locator commissions, concessions,
